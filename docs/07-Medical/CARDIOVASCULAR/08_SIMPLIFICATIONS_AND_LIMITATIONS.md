## Purpose

This document records the educational simplifications and limitations used in the cardiovascular MVP. It keeps the learner informed about what the lesson omits, compresses, or teaches as a convention.

## Simplification principles

- Simplification must never invert the truth of the route.
- The lesson should disclose meaningful omissions without overwhelming the learner.
- Teaching conventions must be distinguishable from literal anatomy.
- Any change to the simplification model requires review.

## Simplifications

| Simplification | Reason | Learner-facing note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major-route model only | Keeps the lesson focused and understandable | “This lesson uses a simplified major-route model.” |
| No full vessel tree | Avoids overload and keeps the route readable | “Only the main route is shown here.” |
| No pressure curves | Exceeds the MVP learning objective | “Pressure detail is outside this lesson.” |
| Broad oxygenation explanation | Supports a clear first pass | “The lungs change the blood’s oxygenation state in this simplified model.” |
| No pathology content | Requires separate review and scope | “This lesson covers normal circulation, not disease.” |

## Limitations

- The model does not show every anatomical branch or variation.
- The lesson does not attempt full clinical detail.
- The lesson should not be interpreted as diagnosis or treatment guidance.
- The visual model is a teaching model, not literal anatomy in every visual detail.

## Review rules

- Simplifications must be visible in the lesson where they matter.
- Limitations should appear in lesson-facing copy or annotation content when relevant.
- Reviewers must confirm that simplifications do not mislead the learner.
